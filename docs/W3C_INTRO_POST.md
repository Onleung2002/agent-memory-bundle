Hello all,

I'm Isaac Leung, based in Hong Kong, with a compliance and risk background in regulated financial services (fund controllership and licensing-regime work).

I joined this group because, seen from a regulated-industry angle, portable and verifiably erasable agent memory is one of the few AI infrastructure problems where the demand signal comes from regulators, not only from developers: GDPR Art. 17/20, the EU AI Act, and regional frameworks (e.g. SFC and HKMA guidance on generative and agentic AI in Hong Kong) are converging on obligations that today's memory stacks cannot produce evidence for. What supervisors ultimately ask for is not a format — it is proof.

I'm working on a neutral conformance effort (a profile on top of existing protocol work, an executable test suite, and erasure-evidence tooling) that builds on this group's and the IETF track's output rather than competing with it.

Two questions I'd appreciate the group's view on:

1. Which deliverable does the group intend to ship first — the baseline interoperability profile or the conformance/test-vector pack?
2. Is there appetite for a regulated-data profile earlier rather than later in the charter's sequencing?

Separately: I can contribute use-case material from regulated financial-services contexts (what supervisors actually accept as erasure/portability evidence, and where current memory architectures fail those tests), if useful for the use-case catalogue.
